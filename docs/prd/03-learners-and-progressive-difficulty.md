## 3 Learners and progressive difficulty

The launch UI offers five starting bands. They establish an initial hypothesis, not a permanent label. A learner may be strong at reading and weak at listening, or fluent in familiar topics and uncertain about formal requests. Adapt those dimensions separately.

| **Starting band** | **Sensei behavior** | **Meaningful challenge** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zero knowledge | English guidance, one new idea at a time, visible reference, Japanese audio, optional kana and romaji. | Recognize an object and make a supported request. |
| Early beginner | Short Japanese with optional English; generous response time; one clear conversational purpose. | Choose an item, express a preference, or ask for repetition. |
| Developing beginner | Simple connected exchanges and one quantity or spatial relationship at a time. | Specify which item is wanted or follow a placement instruction. |
| Intermediate | Japanese-led dialogue, multi-step goals, clarification, reasons, and paraphrases. | Explain a choice and repair a misunderstanding without restarting. |
| Advanced | Sustained Japanese, competing preferences, register, implied meaning, and targeted feedback. | Negotiate a plan, defend it, and reformulate it for a different social relationship. |

An advanced learner is never forced through beginner lessons or unlocks. Higher difficulty requires richer interaction and reasoning, not merely faster audio or uncommon words. Free conversation can cover topics beyond the sprite catalog; visual actions remain limited to supported objects.

Optional self-reported JLPT experience may guide initial context, but the product does not turn that into a speaking score. The official JLPT description states that it does not directly assess speaking or writing. This product uses observed communicative abilities rather than claiming an examination-equivalent level. \[S7\]

### The same object supports different language

- Zero: 水。 / みず / mizu / water. Sensei points to the water item while saying the word.

- Early beginner: 水をください。 / みずをください / Mizu o kudasai. / Water, please.

- Beginner: お茶より水が好きです。 / I prefer water to tea. Sensei asks a short contextual follow-up.

- Intermediate: 冷たい飲み物は苦手なので、常温の水をもらえますか。 / I am not keen on cold drinks; could I have room-temperature water?

- Advanced: discuss how the request should change when speaking to a close friend, a shop employee, or a host, then negotiate an alternative when the requested option is unavailable.

Only properties explicitly represented in the scene may drive a world action. For example, temperature is a scenario property only when the activity has declared it; Sensei must not infer it from a bottle sprite. Conversation can discuss hypothetical properties without changing the authoritative object record.
