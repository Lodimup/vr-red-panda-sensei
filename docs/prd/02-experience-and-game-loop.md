## 2 Experience and game loop

The core session is a conversation with meaningful things to do. The learner may follow Sensei's suggested activity, ask an unrelated Japanese question, or take control of the scene. There is no mandatory quiz wall between the learner and the character.

1. Encounter: Sensei calls tools to arrange two to four catalog objects, start a validated activity, and introduce a useful word, expression, or communicative goal.

2. Communicate: the learner speaks, points, selects, or moves an object; they can ask for help without leaving the activity.

3. See a consequence: Sensei calls the relevant game tool; Unity executes it, so a requested object appears, an item is handed over, or the scene changes in response to meaning.

4. Check: the engine checks the relevant world state and action history; language feedback is attached to the actual utterance.

5. Vary: a fresh arrangement, quantity, intention, or phrasing gives another opportunity with an appropriate amount of support.

6. Remember: the session records useful evidence, identifies what still needed help, and offers a sensible next activity.

### Three activity families

| **Activity** | **Player experience** | **Progression** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discover and request | Ask what an object is, hear its name, express a preference, request an item, and ask a follow-up question. | Naming and short requests become comparison, clarification, explanation, and negotiation. |
| Listen and arrange | Follow spoken instructions or direct Sensei to place objects in the practice area. | One-step placement becomes quantities, exclusions, conditions, order, and conversational repair. |
| Converse and role-play | Talk with Sensei as a helper, host, shopkeeper, or conversation partner using the same objects. | Supported exchanges become sustained discussion, implied constraints, register changes, and nuanced reformulation. |

A default session lasts roughly five to ten minutes, but the learner can continue, stop, or choose conversation only. There are no lives, punitive timers, or forced streaks. A task is an invitation to communicate, not a prerequisite for asking a question.
